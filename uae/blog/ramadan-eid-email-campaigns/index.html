Ramadan and Eid Email Campaign Planning for UAE Stores

Published 9 November 2026EmailFlow

For a UAE ecommerce business choosing to run Ramadan or Eid campaigns, start with operational readiness. A well-timed email needs an approved offer, available stock and a delivery promise your team can meet. Adapt the plan to your audience rather than assuming every subscriber observes the same occasions.

Build the calendar from fulfilment backwards

Ask your fulfilment team for delivery cutoffs by the areas you serve. Record which dates are confirmed and which remain provisional. Avoid hard-coding future religious dates into automated messages until your team has verified the relevant calendar and its implications for the business.

Work backwards from the last realistic order date: allow time for creative review, translation where needed, testing and any stock changes. A campaign about gifting needs a different deadline from an email promoting a digital product or an item available for collection.

Choose a useful message for each stage

  • Preparation: help customers find relevant products, sizes or gift options before the delivery deadline.
  • Consideration: explain product details, genuine customer questions and any offer conditions.
  • Cutoff reminder: state the actual order deadline and covered delivery areas clearly.
  • After the occasion: provide product care or useful follow-up without repeating an expired seasonal promotion.

Use respectful language and imagery that fit your brand and audience. Have someone familiar with the context review the campaign. Relevance comes from helping the customer make a decision, not adding a seasonal greeting to every message.

Check offers in AED

Keep AED totals, minimum spend, discount exclusions and shipping conditions consistent across the email, landing page and checkout. Check whether a promotion combines with an existing welcome offer. Test the code before scheduling and confirm what happens if a featured product sells out.

A modest offer that preserves margin may be more useful than a large discount. Compare expected contribution after product costs, delivery, returns and promotional spend; do not judge the campaign by revenue alone.

Coordinate English and Arabic versions

If your programme includes both languages, assign an approver for each and allow time to test right-to-left layouts. Confirm product names, offer conditions, numbers and delivery wording. Keep a record of customer language preferences so each audience receives the intended version.

EmailFlow’s Arabic copy, translation and right-to-left template work require an agreed scope. Confirm those responsibilities before adding bilingual delivery dates to your campaign calendar.

Schedule around your own audience

Use Gulf Standard Time consistently in your planning and account settings. Test send times against your own audience instead of assuming that one hour works for every UAE customer. Review frequency across scheduled campaigns and automated messages, with exclusions where needed.

Review the full result

Compare campaigns over consistent windows and note differences in offer strength, stock, audience size and delivery coverage. Record clicks, orders, unsubscribes, discounts and attributed revenue. Treat attribution as a reporting method rather than proof of incremental growth.
