Strategy

We use AI. We still look closely at every email.

Published on 2026-10-27 · EmailFlow

Yes, we use AI at EmailFlow. We also care about what we put our name on.

Those two things belong together. AI can help develop an idea, explore different ways to say something and move a draft forward. But a draft that sounds fluent can still be vague, off-brand or simply wrong for the customer receiving it.

When we say we do not do “AI slop”, we mean we will not treat generated output as finished work just because it reads smoothly. We look closely. At the words, the design, the offer and the reason the email exists.

Your brand should survive the draft

You have probably seen the kind of email we mean. “Elevate your everyday.” “Discover your new obsession.” A polished opening, a few broad promises and a button that could belong to almost any shop.

There is nothing useful about sounding professional if the message could be sent by ten different brands with only the logo changed.

Our standard is more specific: what does this product actually do? What might the customer need to know before buying? What would your brand say, and what would it never say? AI can suggest an answer. It cannot be trusted to know your business without context and scrutiny.

Look at the difference in the email itself

Imagine a shop selling a washable wool jumper. A generic draft might say:

Discover unparalleled comfort and timeless style. Elevate your wardrobe with our must-have knitwear collection.

That tells the customer very little. A more useful direction might be:

A wool jumper you can wash at home. Soft enough for everyday wear, with the care instructions right here before you buy.

This is an illustrative example, not a client campaign. The second version would only be usable if the product details and care instructions supported it. A stronger sentence is no improvement if it makes an unsupported promise.

That is the work: find something worth saying, verify it and make it easy to understand.

The same care belongs in the design

An email can look impressive in isolation and still be awkward to use. A headline may be hard to read on a phone. A large image may bury the offer. A button may lead somewhere that does not match the message.

We care about the details that make an email useful: a clear reading order, readable text, product imagery that represents the product accurately and a next step the customer can find.

AI-generated visuals deserve the same scrutiny as copy. If an image changes the shape, colour or features of something you sell, attractive is not enough. The customer needs an honest picture of what they are buying.

How should you compare us with another agency?

Ask to see how the work changes between the first draft and the finished email. Ask why a particular message belongs in your customer journey. Ask who checks the offer, links and product claims.

There are careful agencies using AI, and careful agencies choosing other tools. We do not claim to be the only people who care. The comparison that matters is how much thought reaches the finished work.

What to look atWhat we expect of our work
CopySpecific reasons to care about your product, expressed in your brand's voice.
DesignA clear message and an easy next step, including on a phone.
AutomationA reason for the trigger, timing and audience of each message.
Quality checksAttention to product details, offer conditions, links and the destination page.
ReportingClear explanations of what the numbers show and what they cannot prove.

Those are standards you can use when reviewing any agency, including us. A claim about quality should stand up to looking at the actual work.

More emails is not automatically a better service

AI makes it easier to produce more variations. That does not mean your subscribers need more messages.

A useful welcome series answers the questions a new customer is likely to have. A useful abandoned-cart email helps someone return to their purchase. Each message needs a job beyond filling a slot in a calendar.

We work exclusively with Omnisend. The thinking behind the setup matters as much as the words inside it: who receives the email, when it arrives and whether another message has already said the same thing. Generating another paragraph does not resolve those decisions.

We are responsible for the finished work

“The AI wrote it” is no explanation for an incorrect offer or a sentence that sounds nothing like your brand. Choosing to use a tool also means taking responsibility for what you keep, change or remove.

The same principle applies to reporting. We will not invent a result to make a campaign sound successful, or present platform-attributed revenue as proof that every order was caused by email. Read our guide to email measurement for why that distinction matters.

We use AI because it can help with the work. We look closely because the work represents your business. You should be able to recognise your brand in the result, understand why the message was sent and trust the details inside it.
