How to read these results

This case study reports an individual store’s results. Email-attributed revenue depends on the platform and attribution window; it is not a controlled measure of incremental sales. Client identity is withheld, and this page does not provide an independent verification of the account data. Results are not a forecast for another store. Source reports, attribution windows and click/open credit settings are not supplied on this page. Customer market and changes in traffic, promotions and seasonality are not fully documented, so this comparison cannot isolate their effects.

The situation before

An e-commerce store came to us doing a steady £70–80K a month — entirely from paid ads and organic traffic. Email marketing simply didn't exist: no forms, no emails, no list. Without signup forms and follow-up journeys, the store had fewer opportunities to stay in touch with eligible visitors and customers.

In other words — an opportunity to assess relevant follow-up for visitors and customers, while respecting marketing permissions.

What we did

Honestly — executed through our ecommerce email marketing agency framework on Omnisend:

  1. List building: pop-ups and signup forms — the list started growing from week one;
  2. Automations: welcome series, abandoned cart and abandoned checkout flows, post-purchase emails — our core automated flow strategy set up within the first days;
  3. Regular campaigns: weekly newsletters — offers, news, education;
  4. Ongoing care: deliverability fixes (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), segmentation, monthly reports, and continuous optimization.

What these numbers teach

Automations do the heavy lifting. 87% of email revenue (£39,414 of £45,471) came not from campaigns but from flows running in the background — emails that send automatically when someone abandons a cart, subscribes, or makes a purchase. Automated sending still requires testing, monitoring and updates.

Start with the relevant journeys. Signup forms, flows and regular campaigns were part of this programme. The reports do not isolate the contribution of each implementation change.

Comparison limits: total store revenue increased from £77,845 in August 2025 to £180,716 in April 2026. April email attribution was £45,471 ÷ £180,716 = 25.2% (rounded). This share does not establish incremental revenue or distinguish the effects of email, traffic, advertising or seasonality.
